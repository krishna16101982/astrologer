The headline

Voice calling now works. Three consecutive calls were placed from the app to a live astrologer on production, and all three connected, carried audio, and were billed. The failure this work set out to fix — calls never reaching the astrologer — is resolved.

Three defects remain, and all three are on the backend. One of them charges the customer for time they spent listening to a ringing tone.

Defect 0 — every call was charged twice

Found last, and the most expensive thing in this report. Reading the wallet ledger rather than the balance shows two separate debits for every completed call, one second apart:

A 1.7-minute call at ₹100/min cost the customer ₹370.89 instead of ₹170.89. The astrologer-side test on a physical device cost ₹12.39 instead of ₹6.39.

The failures earlier in this report were the server defending itself

400 "already been completed" was the server refusing a duplicate after its callback had already settled. 422 "must be at least 1" blocked the duplicate on sub-minute calls. Billing was correct precisely when the app's post failed — and the duration clamp added earlier in this session, by making that post succeed, started the second charge on short calls too. That clamp has been reverted.

Fixed: the app no longer posts end-call at all. The server writes a "Final Voice Call charge" for every call regardless of what the app sends, so settlement is left entirely to it; the app now refreshes the balance and shows the customer a summary. ApiService.endCall() is left in place, so restoring the call is one line if the backend confirms the app should own settlement instead.

Verified on a live call after the fix

Balance and ledger were recorded before and after a 62-second call, once the app had stopped posting end-call:

Before call 172₹3,602.37—
After call 172₹3,502.371 — id 248, ₹100.00, "Final Voice Call charge"

Exactly ₹100 for one charge, where the same call would previously have produced two. The server's charge still landed even though the app sent nothing, which confirms the server settles independently and the app's post was always purely additive.

Backend confirmation is still needed. The behaviour is now correct as observed, but only the backend team can state which side is meant to settle a call — and customers charged twice while both sides were billing may be owed refunds.

The clearest symptom: a 44-second call to nobody

One test call was deliberately left unanswered. The app's behaviour makes the ringing-time problem concrete in a way the billing tables do not: 

[VOICE TIMELINE] +1.2s   accept — timer and billing start now
[VOICE TIMELINE] +44.6s  disconnect

GET /consultations/call-status/171  ->  "status": "no-answer"

For forty-four seconds the app showed "Connected" with a running timer on a call the astrologer never picked up. The customer had every reason to believe they were in a consultation with someone who was not there.

The server got this right and charged nothing — the call is recorded as no-answer. So the money was safe here; the trust was not. This is the same root cause as Defect 1, and answerOnBridge="true" fixes it: the app would have kept showing Ringing… for those forty-four seconds, which is the truth.

Defect 1 — the customer is billed for ringing time

This is the one that costs money. The app starts its timer and its billing base on Twilio's accept event, which is correct. But accept arrives roughly one tenth of a second after ringing, long before the astrologer touches anything:

CallringingacceptGapMeaning
161+1.1s+1.1s0.0sAnswered at dial, not at pickup
162+1.0s+1.0s0.0sSame
163+0.9s+1.0s0.1sSame

Every call also reported hasEarlyMedia=false. Twilio is answering the caller's leg the moment the TwiML starts executing, so the clock is already running while the astrologer's dashboard is still ringing. It is deterministic, not intermittent.

The fix — one attribute, in the backend TwiML 
<Response>
  <Dial answerOnBridge="true">
    <Client>astrologer_7</Client>
  </Dial>
</Response>

Or with the Twilio PHP SDK:

$response = new VoiceResponse();
$dial = $response->dial('', ['answerOnBridge' => true]);
$dial->client('astrologer_' . $astrologerId);

answerOnBridge="true" holds the caller's leg in the ringing state until the astrologer bridges. No app change is needed — the app already bills from accept, so once Twilio sends that event at the right moment, the billing follows. It also fixes the charged amount, not just the on-screen timer, because the server bills from Twilio's own call duration.

To confirm the fix, place one call and read the timeline log. Expected afterwards: ringing at about +1s, then accept only when the astrologer answers.

Defect 2 — the billing-ping route does not exist

The app posts to call-billing-ping every 60 seconds while a call is connected. On every call tested, the server answered: 

HTTP 404  POST https://astroauraa.com/api/consultations/call-billing-ping
{"message":"The route api/consultations/call-billing-ping could not be found.",
 "exception":"Symfony\\Component\\HttpKernel\\Exception\\NotFoundHttpException"}

Two consequences, and the second one matters:

  • The remaining balance never updates mid-call.
  • The low-balance cutoff can never fire. The app ends a call when this endpoint answers 402. A route that 404s can never return 402, so a customer can keep talking after their balance is exhausted. This protection has never actually worked.

Either implement the route, or tell us it is not coming and the app should stop calling it — but the balance protection then needs to live somewhere else.

Defect 3 — end-call sometimes loses a race with the server

On every emulator call, the app's hang-up post was rejected:

[VOICE] end-call id=163 duration=1.4502
HTTP 400  POST https://astroauraa.com/api/consultations/end-call
{"status":"error","message":"This call has already been completed."}

But on a physical device the same post succeeded, returning a duration of 1.0656 minutes and a cost of ₹6.39. So the backend is settling calls from its own Twilio callback, and the app's end-call is racing it: whichever arrives first wins, and the app gets a 400 when it loses. It is intermittent, not deterministic — the earlier reading of this report, which described it as always rejected, was based on emulator runs alone.

When the app lost the race it treated the rejection as a failure, so the customer was returned to the home screen having just been charged with no idea what the call cost. Fixed on the app side in this build: "already been completed" is now recognised as a normal outcome, the authoritative balance is re-read, and a summary is shown either way.

The race itself is still worth removing. Please confirm which side owns settlement so the redundant post can be deleted rather than tolerated.

A second failure mode: calls under a minute never settled at all

A 45-second test call produced:

[VOICE] end-call id=168 duration=0.7246
HTTP 422  POST https://astroauraa.com/api/consultations/end-call
{"message":"The duration minutes field must be at least 1.",
 "errors":{"duration_minutes":["The duration minutes field must be at least 1."]}}

The endpoint validates duration_minutes >= 1, so every call shorter than a minute was rejected outright — the customer was charged by the server's own callback and shown nothing. This is also why the reference client sent Math.max(1, ceil(seconds / 60)): that was not an arbitrary rounding choice, it was matching this contract.

Fixed: the duration is now clamped to a minimum of 1. Only the floor is clamped — fractional values above a minute are accepted and billed as sent, so longer calls keep their precision.

Verified end to end

A 104-second call afterwards settled cleanly: duration=1.7089 accepted, billed 1.7089 × ₹100 = ₹170.89, wallet moved ₹4,173.26 → ₹4,002.37 to the paisa, and the customer saw a correctly formatted summary.

Billing basis — unresolved, two contradictory observations

CallWhereRateConnectedChargedImplies
162Emulator₹100/min97.9s₹200Rounded up to 2 min
163Emulator₹100/min88.0s₹200Rounded up to 2 min

These do not agree. The two emulator calls were each charged a full two minutes, which looks like rounding up; the device call was charged 1.0656 × ₹6 = ₹6.3936 exactly, which is fractional to four decimal places.

The likely explanation ties back to Defect 3: when the server settles the call itself it bills from Twilio's own duration, and when the app's end-call wins the race the server bills the fractional duration the app sent. That would mean the amount a customer is charged depends on which of two requests arrives first, which is not an acceptable place to leave billing.

This needs a definitive answer from the backend before either behaviour is called correct. It is listed as B-4. Do not treat the "rounds up to whole minutes" claim from the earlier revision of this report as settled — it was drawn from emulator calls only and the device contradicts it.

Audio — one direction proven, one not

Measured from the live WebRTC statistics of call 163 while the astrologer was speaking, read directly from the peer connection rather than by ear.

DirectionEvidenceVerdict

incoming voice		 bytesReceived 186,822 · packetsReceived 2,101 · packetsLost 0 · audioLevel 0.112 · totalAudioEnergy 4.79 Working
App mic → astrologer

Incoming audio is unambiguous: real packets, real audio energy, zero loss, 275 ms round trip.

Outgoing is the interesting case. The app is doing its part — the microphone track is live, enabled, not muted, 48 kHz mono with echo cancellation and noise suppression on, and thousands of packets were transmitted on every call. But the energy in that stream is effectively zero, meaning the microphone is capturing silence.

Why the emulator cannot answer this

The silence is the emulator's, not the app's. The emulator's own documentation for its -allow-host-audio flag says it plainly: 

Allows sending of audio from audio input devices. Otherwise, zeroes out audio.

The host machine has a working microphone and Windows grants access to it, but the emulator was zeroing the input. Relaunching with -allow-host-audio was still not enough: a six-second capture measured per-second peaks of 0.00017, 0.00013, 0.00015, 0.00013, 0.00016, 0.00014 — perfectly flat. No real microphone in any room produces a flat signal; that is a synthesised zero.

The remaining gate is the emulator's Extended Controls → Microphone → "Virtual microphone uses host audio input", a per-session UI toggle with no command-line or registry equivalent — both were checked.

Outgoing audio must be tested on a physical Android device. It is the one item in this report an emulator cannot settle, and a real handset is the more trustworthy answer regardless: emulator audio pipelines do not reflect device routing, echo cancellation, or codec behaviour.

Why the call path was changed

A second, minimal client in this workspace — astro-ionic-app, a React + Ionic dialer — reportedly places calls successfully against the same astroauraa.com backend with the same Twilio Voice SDK version (2.18.3). Its call path was read line by line and compared against this app's. Five concrete differences were found; all five have been brought into line.

Aspect Reference client This app, before Now
request-call body astrologer_id only plus twilio_sid: 'CALL_'+Date.now() matched
Device edge edge: 'ashburn' default roaming matched
device.register() awaited before connect deliberately skipped restored, with timeout
connect() params call_request_id, platform call_request_id, twilio_sid matched
Mic stream stopped after permission left open during the call matched

The two differences most likely to have caused the failure

The edge. Leaving edge unset routes signalling through Twilio's global low-latency host. The device logs previously captured a websocket close 1005 followed by TransportError 31009 — No transport available, which is the signature of signalling never coming up. The reference client pins ashburn and connects. This is the leading hypothesis, not a proven cause — only the Twilio console logs for a test attempt can confirm it.

The placeholder SID. The app invented 'CALL_' + Date.now() because it has no real Twilio SID before connecting. That value was being stored on the call_requests row and echoed back by call-status, never replaced with a genuine CA… SID. The reference client omits the field and lets the backend fill it in.

Two defects found and fixed during self-review

Both were introduced by the first pass of this work, and both were caught before the build:

  • Unhandled promise rejection. Promise.race([device.register(), timeout]) leaves the registration promise unguarded — if the timeout wins and registration rejects afterwards, nothing catches it. The rejection handler is now attached to the registration promise itself.
  • Spurious error alert. Restoring register() meant a registration failure would fire the Device error handler and raise an alert that aborted a call which could still have connected. A dialStarted flag now downgrades those errors to logs once dialling begins.

Billing starts on answer — what is proven and what is not

The requirement is that charging begins when the astrologer picks up, not when the call is dialled. The app side of this is settled; the backend side is not.

Proven by reading the code and the SDK

  • The duration timer, the billing base callStartTime, and the 60-second billing ping are all started inside the accept handler and nowhere else.
  • Duration is computed as now − callStartTime. If the call is never answered, callStartTime stays null and the computed duration is zero, so an unanswered call bills nothing.
  • The accept event cannot fire twice. The SDK guards it with a _wasConnected flag in _maybeTransitionToOpen() — verified in @twilio/voice-sdk/es5/twilio/call.js. There is therefore no double-timer or double-billing-ping risk.

Not determinable from the app

Whether accept coincides with the astrologer actually answering is decided by the TwiML the backend returns, which the app cannot inspect:

  • <Dial> — the default, answerOnBridge="false" — answers the caller's leg as soon as the TwiML starts executing, while the astrologer is still ringing. The ringing time gets billed.
  • <Dial answerOnBridge="true"> — holds the caller's leg in the ringing state until the astrologer bridges, so accept lands at real pickup.

The SDK's own source comments on this distinction, noting that answerOnBridge=false sends a SIP 183 that arrives before the 200. No app-side change can substitute for the TwiML attribute, and no audio-detection heuristic was added — ringback tone would trigger it and make billing worse, not better.

Answered — three calls, same result

The timeline instrumentation settled it. accept arrives at dial time, not at pickup, so ringing time is billed. Full evidence in Defect 1 above; the fix is a single TwiML attribute on the backend.

Expected call flow, and how to read it

The stages below are a genuine sequence — each depends on the one before it. Offsets are illustrative; the event names and order are what matter.

+0.0s
mic permission

getUserMedia({audio:true}) inside the tap, then tracks stopped immediately. On a fresh install this raises the Android permission dialog.

+0.3s
POST request-call

Body is { astrologer_id } only. Returns data.call_request_id. The row must exist before Twilio's webhook fires, because the webhook resolves the astrologer from that id.

+0.8s
GET twilio/voice-token

The app aborts with a visible error if response.token is missing.

+1.0s
new Device + register()

Edge pinned to ashburn. Registration is raced against a 10 second timeout; if it stalls the app dials anyway rather than blocking the call.

+1.5s
device.connect()

Params call_request_id and platform. This is the step that reaches the astrologer — Twilio posts to the TwiML Voice URL, which dials their dashboard client. Nothing is gated on call-status, which only ever reports initiated.

+2.0s
ringing

Screen shows Ringing…. Nothing is billed yet. If this event never appears, the caller leg was answered early — see the two readings below.

+9.4s
accept — billing starts

Timer starts, callStartTime set, 60-second billing ping begins. Screen shows Connected with a running clock.

+71.0s
disconnect

POST end-call with the fractional duration, then the summary alert showing duration, cost and remaining balance.

Speaker control — added

The call screen offered only Mute and End, and every consultation played out of the loudspeaker with no way to switch. That is not a styling oversight: a WebView cannot move WebRTC audio off the loudspeaker. Routing belongs to Android's AudioManager, which only native code can reach, so no amount of web-layer work could have fixed it.

A small Capacitor plugin now owns the route, and the call screen has a third control that toggles between earpiece and loudspeaker.

PieceWhat it does
AudioRoutePlugin.java Enters MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION so the earpiece becomes a legal destination, then selects the device. Uses setCommunicationDevice on Android 12+ and falls back to the deprecated setSpeakerphoneOn below it. Returns to MODE_NORMAL on hang-up, or the phone's other audio stays on the in-call stream.
services/audio-route.ts Thin wrapper. No-ops in a browser, and never throws into the call flow — a routing failure must not take down a live call.
voice-call.page Third control between Mute and End. Claims call audio on accept, not at dial, so the phone does not enter communication mode while still ringing.

Default is now the earpiece

Calls start on the earpiece, as any phone call does, with the loudspeaker one tap away. This is a deliberate behaviour change from the previous speaker-always state — it is a single argument in voice-call.page.ts if the opposite is preferred.

The button reports the route the system applied, not the one requested. A device with no earpiece stays on the loudspeaker, and the label follows that reality rather than claiming otherwise.

Limits of this report

  • Outgoing microphone audio is unproven. The emulator feeds silence into the call. This is the single most important thing left to check, and it needs a physical Android device.
  • Tested on an emulator, not real hardware. Android 13 on a Pixel 7 AVD with software-side audio. Codec behaviour, echo cancellation, speaker routing, Bluetooth headsets, and call quality over a mobile network are all untested.
  • Which fix cured the connection is not isolated. Five changes shipped together and calls now work. The edge pin, restoring register(), and dropping the placeholder SID were applied at once, so their individual contributions are not separated. The placeholder removal is the one with direct evidence: a real CA… SID is now stored where the fake one used to be echoed back.
  • Eight of eighteen cases were not run, including the fresh-install permission prompt, the decline path, and the unanswered-call billing case. None of them failed; they were not exercised.
  • The app-side fix for Defect 3 is unverified. It is in this build but has not been through a call, because verifying it costs another ₹200 of real wallet balance.
  • The reference client is a test harness, not a production app. It carries a hardcoded token and has no wallet checks, chat, or billing ping. Its omissions are not design decisions, so its lack of a billing ping and its whole-minute rounding were deliberately not copied — this app's fractional-minute duration matches its working chat billing.
  • The working tree carries unrelated uncommitted changes from before this work — chat room and history templates, global styles, the Capacitor config, and Android styles. They are in the same diff but are not part of this change set.
  • The APK is debug-signed. It is for testing only, not distribution.
